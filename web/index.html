Vitruve

Craniofacial measurements taken from a photograph, each reported with the interval it is known to within. Everything happens on this machine.

Plate 01

Capture

Stand about 1.5 m back and zoom in rather than stepping closer. At 0.3 m the nose plane is magnified about 17% relative to the eye plane; at 1.5 m it is about 3%.

Fill the ring, keep both pupils on the horizon line, and hold the camera at eye height. Look at the lens, not at the screen.

Head rotation is the dominant error source here. Kleinberg and Vanezis (2007) photographed subjects in ten-degree steps and watched facial indices move 8 to 19 percent, against a between-subject spread of 1.2 percent for the tightest index. The overlay is the only place in the system where that can be fixed.

Plate 02

Subject

Declared by you, and used only to choose a normative stratum and an interpupillary prior. Nothing here is predicted from the photograph.

Leaving a field empty selects the pooled distribution and widens the intervals. Declaring sex narrows the millimetre intervals by roughly a tenth, because the interpupillary prior tightens from 63.36 ± 3.83 mm to a sex-conditioned one.

A graduated ruler held in the plane of the face replaces the population scale prior with a direct observation. It is the difference between a millimetre value that carries 5.5% prior error and one that carries your reading error.

Plate 03

Report

Every value carries its 95% interval, drawn to scale. A measurement that moves further between two photographs of you than it moves between different people is listed with the reason instead of the number.

FISWG 2026 (V2.1, section 6.4.1) prohibits photo-anthropometry for identification. Vitruve keeps the measurements and gates them.

Colophon

Vitruve is Apache-2.0. Model weights carry their own licenses, listed at /licenses. The default stack is YuNet (MIT), SPIGA (BSD-3), MediaPipe (Apache-2.0) and 6DRepNet (MIT).

Photographs are held in memory and are not written to disk. EXIF, including GPS, is dropped at ingest. The API is documented here and the measurement catalogue is served at /catalogue.
