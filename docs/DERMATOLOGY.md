# The dermatological findings layer

Read this before adding anything to `src/vitruve/derm/`. The design of this
package is almost entirely determined by one fact, so it is stated first.

## The data situation

Consumer skin reports claim four or five findings. Public annotated data exists
for one and a half of them.

| Finding | Public annotated data | Licence | What Vitruve does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acne lesions | **Yes.** ACNE04 (~1,457 images, ~18,983 boxes, Hayashi grade 0-3); several Roboflow Universe detection sets | ACNE04 is "free for academic usage", not an OSI licence. Roboflow sets vary; some are **CC BY 4.0** | Detected with YOLO instance segmentation over region crops, behind `Tier.COPYLEFT` |
| Wrinkles / folds | **Yes.** FFHQ-Wrinkle: 1,000 human-labelled pixel masks plus 50,000 weak masks | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | Not implemented. The only real masks are non-commercial, so this cannot sit on the default path |
| Periorbital hyperpigmentation ("dark circles") | **None.** No dataset, no credible model | n/a | **Measured** as a paired infraorbital-minus-malar contrast in CIELAB |
| Erythema | **None.** | n/a | **Measured** as an a* elevation against paired reference skin |
| Pores | **None.** | n/a | Not implemented, and not faked |
| Skin tone | n/a (this is a measurement, not a detection) | n/a | Individual Typology Angle plus nearest Monk Skin Tone swatch |

Two further traps sit next to the data:

* **Face parsers are not free.** `jonathandinu/face-parsing`, every BiSeNet
  port, and every "face parsing" checkpoint on the hub descend from
  CelebAMask-HQ, whose terms forbid reproducing, selling or trading any portion
  of the derived data. Several of these carry an MIT tag. The tag covers the
  code. It does not cover the weights.
* **Ultralytics asserts AGPL-3.0 over the models its trainer produces**, not
  only over its code. A "yolov8-acne" checkpoint tagged MIT is a relabel, and
  the relabel does not remove the obligation.

## Why measuring beats detecting, where there is no data

The tempting move for dark circles and erythema is to train a small model on
something. There is nothing to train it on, so in practice such a model is
trained on a private set, or on weak labels generated by a heuristic, and either
way the published claim is unfalsifiable.

The alternative is not weaker. It is stronger, for a specific reason.

An absolute threshold on redness or darkness is a statement about the
illuminant, the camera's white balance and the subject's constitutive
pigmentation before it is a statement about the skin. It fails hardest on dark
skin, where the same absolute a* elevation corresponds to a much larger
perceptual change. A **difference between two regions of the same face in the
same photograph** cancels the illuminant, the camera response to first order,
and the person's own baseline tone. What survives is the thing being asked
about.

So:

* **Erythema** is `a*(target) - a*(reference)`, with the default pairing malar
  against lateral cheek. a* is the red-green opponent axis and cutaneous
  erythema is haemoglobin absorption in the green band, so a* is the channel
  that moves.
* **Periorbital hyperpigmentation** is the infraorbital-minus-ipsilateral-malar
  difference in **L\* and b\*, reported separately**. Both channels moving
  (darker and yellower) is the pattern pigment produces. L* alone, with no b*
  shift, is what shadow from the orbital rim or from tear-trough volume loss
  produces. Collapsing them into one number would hide the distinction that
  matters most.
* **Skin tone** is the Individual Typology Angle, `atan((L* - 50) / b*)` in
  degrees, plus the nearest Monk Skin Tone swatch in CIELAB.

`tests/unit/test_colorimetry.py` plants a known contrast in a synthetic image,
applies a known illuminant cast, and checks that the absolute colour moves by
more than 4 CIELAB units while the paired contrast moves by less than one. That
test is the argument.

### Monk, not Fitzpatrick

Fitzpatrick was built in 1975 to predict erythemal response to UVA dosing, by
asking light-skinned patients how they burned. Types V and VI were appended
later and between them absorb the entire range of dark skin. It was never a
scale of appearance, and computer-vision fairness audits that use it inherit its
compression.

Monk's ten swatches were built for appearance, and in a 2,214-person US survey
(Heldreth et al. 2024, *ACM Journal on Responsible Computing*) were rated more
representative than the alternatives by participants across skin tones.

The compression is measurable rather than rhetorical. Converting the ten
published Monk swatches to CIELAB and binning them by the conventional ITA
bands, the three darkest swatches all land in a single "dark" bin and the four
lightest all land in "very light" -- so the ITA bands resolve six classes where
Monk resolves ten. `test_ita_bands_collapse_dark_skin_where_monk_does_not`
asserts exactly that.

Skin tone here selects a stratum for reporting measurement error. It is never
itself a finding about a person, and it is never inferred as a demographic
attribute.

## White balance, stated every time

An uncalibrated consumer photograph has no colour reference in it, so its
absolute L\*a\*b\* is unanchored. The rule the package enforces:

* **Paired contrasts** are comparable within one photograph. They survive an
  uncalibrated white balance, because the cast is common to both regions.
* **Absolute values** (ITA, Monk tone) are *not* comparable between
  photographs. Every one of them carries `ABSOLUTE_UNCALIBRATED_NOTE` and is
  downgraded to a caveat until a reference is present.
* `grey_card_balance(patch)` computes real von Kries gains in linear light from
  a neutral card in frame. Passing `reflectance=0.18` also anchors exposure,
  which is what makes absolute L\* comparable between photographs rather than
  merely hue-correct. Without it the hue is calibrated and the lightness is not,
  and the returned notes say so.

## Uncertainty, and the withholding rule

Every contrast carries a standard error, and a contrast smaller than its own
standard error is **withheld with a reason rather than printed**. This is the
discriminability rule of `core/sensitivity.py` applied to a colour difference:
a quantity that cannot be distinguished from the noise in its own measurement is
not a result.

The standard error is computed from the spread of **8-pixel block means**, not
from the pixel count. Neighbouring skin pixels are not independent samples: the
same shading gradient, the same lens blur and the same JPEG macroblock run
through all of them. A pixel-count standard error is optimistic by roughly the
block side, and that inflation is exactly what turns a shading gradient into a
"significant" finding. `test_cluster_standard_error_exceeds_the_pixel_one_on_correlated_data`
plants a shading ramp and checks the block estimator sees it.

A floor of 0.5 CIELAB units sits under every standard error. Eight-bit
quantisation, JPEG chroma subsampling and demosaicing put a hard bottom under
how finely colour can be read out of a consumer photograph, and one CIELAB unit
is roughly the classical just-noticeable difference.

A withheld finding **bounds** its value without restating it. The reason reads
"the a* contrast is below its own standard error of 0.71", never the number.

## Regions: geometry, not a parser

`derm/regions.py` derives thirteen regions from the landmark set as polygons in
a rigid in-plane frame: origin between the pupils, `u` toward the subject's
right, `v` up, every offset in units of the interpupillary span.

That buys three things:

* **Roll invariance.** The frame rotates with the eye line, so a tilted
  photograph gives the same regions rotated, not the same rectangles.
* **Scale invariance.** One offset table works at any resolution.
* **Named failure.** A region whose landmarks are missing lands in
  `RegionSet.unavailable` with those landmarks named. A parser instead returns a
  confident mask over whatever it hallucinated.

Regions: forehead, glabella, T-zone, nasal, periorbital L/R, infraorbital L/R,
malar L/R, lateral cheek L/R, perioral. The perioral polygon carries the
vermilion as an explicit **hole**, because lip a* runs far above any cheek and
including it would make every perioral erythema contrast read positive.

What the geometric path gives up is boundary fidelity: a polygon does not know
where hair, eyebrow or nostril are. Two mitigations. Colour samples are eroded
12% toward the centroid before they are taken, and
`refine_with_parser(regions, parse, allowed_tier=Tier.NONCOMMERCIAL)` will
intersect every region with a parser-derived skin mask for callers who accept
that tier. The geometric path works alone, and the tests prove it.

### The residual confound

The lateral cheek curves away from a frontal light source, so it is darker than
the malar apex, and CIELAB chroma is not perfectly lightness-independent. That
puts a small illumination-geometry term into every malar-minus-lateral contrast.
It is small next to a real erythema and it is not zero, so it is stated in the
finding's notes rather than corrected away.

## Acne detection: where YOLO genuinely belongs

Acne is genuinely multi-instance -- zero to a hundred separate lesions, each a
few pixels across, each needing its own box and confidence. That is the problem
class one-stage detectors were designed for, and it is the only place in Vitruve
where YOLO is the right tool rather than the familiar one. Dense landmarking is
not detection and is served better by heatmaps, which is why YOLO does not
appear there.

**Crop, then detect.** A 4000 px selfie fed to a 640 px network is downsampled
by six, and a 12 px papule becomes two pixels. Detection therefore runs over
region crops at native resolution, which also means every detection arrives
already attributed to a named region. Overlapping tiles are merged afterwards by
IoU, because a lesion on a tile boundary would otherwise be counted twice, and
the output here is a count.

A detection is kept only when its **centre falls inside the region polygon**.
The tile is a rectangle and the region is not, so without that test a forehead
tile would claim lesions found in the hair.

Periorbital regions are excluded from detection: eyelashes and lid margins
generate false positives at the rate that matters, and acne does not occur on
the eyelid in any quantity worth the noise.

### Severity

Hayashi et al. (2008, *Journal of Dermatology*) graded acne by counting
**inflammatory lesions on one half of the face**: 0-5 mild, 6-20 moderate,
21-50 severe, above 50 very severe. ACNE04 grades to that convention.

Two consequences the code states explicitly every time it prints a grade:

1. A detector inherits its training set's definition of a countable lesion, so
   the grade is calibrated on **ACNE04's grading convention**, not on a
   clinician's examination.
2. The grade is defined on a half face. `AcneDetection.hayashi_count(midline_x)`
   splits every lesion by its own position against the facial midline, including
   lesions in midline regions, because a forehead spans both halves. Without a
   midline the code halves the whole-face count, rounds up, and says in the
   finding's reasons that it did.

A lesion count is a Poisson draw, so `n` carries a standard error of `sqrt(n)`.
That is the interval printed. Detector recall is a second, larger, unmeasured
term unless the model card records a stratified evaluation, and the finding is
caveated until it does.

## Licence tiers

| Capability | Tier | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Geometric regions | `PERMISSIVE` | Pure geometry over landmarks. No weights |
| Colorimetry: erythema, pigmentation, ITA, Monk tone | `PERMISSIVE` | Arithmetic on pixels. No weights, no training data |
| Acne detection and Hayashi grading | `COPYLEFT` | Ultralytics asserts AGPL-3.0 over models its trainer produces. No permissive equivalent exists |
| Face-parser region refinement | `NONCOMMERCIAL` | Every public face parser descends from CelebAMask-HQ |
| Wrinkle segmentation | not implemented | FFHQ-Wrinkle is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 |

`licensing.require()` is called **before** the import and before any weight is
opened, in both `AcneDetector.load()` and `regions.load_face_parser()`. A check
that runs after `from_pretrained` has already downloaded a research-only
checkpoint has not prevented anything.

## Training the acne detector

`python -m vitruve.derm.train` fine-tunes against a CC BY 4.0 export and writes
`model_card.json` and `MODEL_CARD.md` next to the weights.

1. Pick a Roboflow Universe set whose licence line reads exactly `CC BY 4.0`.
   Check the licence on the **version** page, not the project page.
2. Export in `YOLOv8` format. You get `data.yaml` plus `train/`, `valid/`,
   `test/`.
3. Keep the citation Roboflow generates. It is the attribution CC BY requires
   and it is a **required argument**, not an optional one.
4. Run:

```bash
python -m vitruve.derm.train \
  --data /path/to/export/data.yaml \
  --dataset-name "pimples-detection v14" \
  --dataset-license CC-BY-4.0 \
  --dataset-url https://universe.roboflow.com/charan-a-a-8lhwj/pimples-detection/dataset/14 \
  --dataset-citation "Pimples detection dataset, charan-a-a-8lhwj, Roboflow Universe, v14 (2023-08-04), CC BY 4.0." \
  --license-tier copyleft --task detect --base-weights yolov8n.pt \
  --out runs/acne-v1
```

The script refuses in three situations, each with one sentence and exit code 2:

* `--license-tier` below `copyleft`, because the trainer produces an AGPL-3.0
  model whatever the dataset says;
* a research-only dataset licence (ACNE04's terms, CC BY-NC) without
  `--accept-noncommercial`;
* a licence string it does not recognise, because guessing what a licence
  permits is the failure mode this whole file exists to avoid.

`--dry-run` resolves the config, runs the licence checks and prints the model
card without training.

The model card records the dataset licence, the citation, a manifest digest over
`data.yaml` and the sorted label filenames of each split, the seed, and the
grading convention. A card that records the metrics but not the data hash cannot
support the claim that a rerun produced the same detector, which is the only
reason to write one.

### A known-good dataset

`KhaMinh/my-acne-dataset` on Hugging Face mirrors the Roboflow export of
**Pimples detection v14** (`charan-a-a-8lhwj/pimples-detection`), 4,621 images
in YOLO format across 10 lesion classes. Its `README.dataset.txt` and the
`roboflow.license` key in `data.yaml` both read `CC BY 4.0`. Cite it wherever a
model trained on it is used.

A 3-epoch smoke run over a 200-image subset at 320 px on CPU reached box mAP50
0.110 and mAP50-95 0.030 on 302 validation instances. That number is a
statement that the pipeline runs end to end, and nothing else. Do not quote it.

## What this layer will not do

* **No recommendations.** No products, no routines, no "consider seeing a
  dermatologist". `findings.contains_advice` is checked in `Finding.__post_init__`,
  so a renderer cannot smuggle advice in through a note. Advice is the part of a
  consumer skin report with the least measurement basis and the clearest path to
  harm.
* **No overall skin score.** Same rule as the measurement layer, same reason.
  `test_no_finding_field_is_an_aggregate_score` enforces it.
* **No diagnosis.** `findings.DISCLAIMER` travels with every `FindingSet`. A
  colour difference between two regions of skin is a property of the image; what
  causes it is outside what this software can determine.

## Tests

```
pytest tests/unit/test_regions.py tests/unit/test_colorimetry.py tests/unit/test_findings.py
```

No weights, no network. Colorimetry tests build their images by inverting the
colour transform with an implementation written independently in the test file,
so a patch built to be L\* 58, a\* 14, b\* 18 has to measure back as that. The
published anchors are asserted directly: sRGB white is L\* 100, mid-grey 128 is
L\* 53.585, and pure sRGB red is (53.24, 80.09, 67.20).
